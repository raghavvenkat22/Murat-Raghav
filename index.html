What the data tells us:
  • The ads are getting clicks. The website is not converting them. CTR has held between 2.5% and 3.3% for most of the year, which is healthy for the category. But only 1 in every 400 visitors buys (0.25% CVR YTD). The funnel breaks after the click, not before it.
  • Paid media is not yet profitable. Year to date, TRY 280.7K of spend has returned TRY 261.0K in purchase value (0.93x). September was the best month at 1.33x.
  • Traffic is getting more expensive. CPM has gone from TRY 122 in April to between TRY 277 and 379 from June to September. When each click costs more, a weak conversion rate gets more costly.
  • Shoppers drop off before checkout. Of 2,121 add-to-carts, only 153 went on to initiate checkout (7%). Closing this gap is one of the quickest wins available.

3. What we should do
A. Website: redesign for trust and conversion
This is the single biggest lever on conversion and should come first.
  • Redesign with social proof built in. Product pages need customer reviews with photos, star ratings on listing and product pages, real customer and UGC imagery, and clearly visible trust signals: secure Shopier payment, 100% anti-allergic material, same-day shipping and the gift-box experience. For a jewellery buyer seeing the brand for the first time, trust is the deciding factor.
  • Price testing. We will run structured tests on price points, bundles (for example, ring stacks and ring + necklace sets) and an entry-level product range. The goal is to find the price architecture that opens the brand to a larger addressable market without eroding margin.
  • Landing page testing with Microsoft Clarity. Heatmaps, scroll depth, session recordings and rage-click data will show us where shoppers hesitate and drop off. Every design change will be driven by that evidence, not by opinion.
B. Creatives: built to convert, not just to get clicks
On Meta today, creative decides targeting. The algorithm finds the audience from the creative itself, so creative quality is now the main targeting lever.
  • Stronger catalog ads. Catalog ads have the highest CTR in the account (around 6%) but have produced one purchase on TRY 16.9K of spend. This is where I can add value directly: our in-house tool, Gavin, converts static product images into video and applies catalog overlays (price, offer, key benefits), making these ads more engaging and giving shoppers what they need to decide.
  • Mid-funnel video. Visitors who have not yet purchased need dedicated video: close-up product detail, try-on and styling, gift-box unboxing and customer stories. Video to warm audiences already returns about 2x, so this is a proven area to invest in.
  • Build on what is already working. Close-up shots of rings on the hand, set against textured backgrounds, are the strongest visuals in the account. Outfit-led fashion shots have not converted. New creative should extend the winning approach, on a regular refresh cycle to prevent fatigue.
C. Targeting
  • Lean into broad targeting powered by creative, with Advantage+ in prospecting, supported by lookalikes built from purchasers and high-intent visitors.
  • Separate audiences by funnel stage so each person sees the message that fits where they are: discovery, consideration or purchase.
D. Campaign structure
  • A full-funnel campaign structure, with each stage (acquisition, consideration, conversion, retention) given its own objective, audiences and creative. I will share the detailed blueprint in the first week.
  • Budget split: 80% acquisition, 20% remarketing and retention. The split is less important than what feeds each stage: acquisition gets creative built to introduce the brand, and remarketing gets creative built to close the sale.
  • Clear test-and-scale rules for every campaign, so budget moves to winners based on data rather than gut feel.
E. Retention and recovery: winning back people who nearly bought
  • A dedicated recovery programme for people who added to cart or started checkout but did not buy, run across Meta remarketing, SMS, WhatsApp and email.
  • Offer sequencing (for example free shipping, a time-bound discount, or a gift-wrapping incentive) to see which nudge works best.
  • Post-purchase flows for repeat orders, reviews and referrals. These also feed the social proof on the website.
Once the website, creative and campaign structure are working together, conversion improves first and profitable scale follows. Let me know if you would like to go deeper on any of these before we begin.
